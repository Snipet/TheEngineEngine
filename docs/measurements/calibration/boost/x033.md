On this tree **the term is the design gas temperature, 900 K for a diesel.** The speed share does not matter.

**What the click is (measured on the two recordings, the package against 1150 K):**
- The largest steps recur every 20 ms around 5.0-5.13 s: a six's firing at about 1000 rpm after the upshift. Their
  size is 0.10-0.14 on the package against 0.09-0.11 at 1150 K.
- The background differs more. The 10 ms RMS is 0.107-0.137 on the package against 0.134-0.177 at 1150 K, some 20 %
  quieter.
- So the metric (a step over the local level) crosses its bound because the firing pulses stand out of a quieter
  background. No discontinuity of the boost machine's appears: the steps are at the firing rate in both.

**The 900 K is not this engine's either.** Measured T3 at full load with boost.model 1 is 581 / 699 / 736 / 689 K at
1200 / 1600 / 2000 / 2400 rpm. Matched at its own T3 the nozzle would be smaller again (sqrt(700/900) = 0.88), the
direction that fails here. So correcting the design temperature would not cure the click.

**Suspected, not proven:** a smaller turbine raises the back pressure the cylinders blow down against and lowers the
exhaust's broadband level after the upshift. Why the pulses sharpen is not measured.

**For the lead:** the question is whether a diesel's firing pulse against a quiet background is a click (a defect) or
the sound itself. That is the metric's owner's and the voices' call, not the match's. The large diesel with
boost.model 1 is also weak low down (411 N m at 1200 rpm, 8 kPa): the design-point match's middle-speed loss, as
5c85736b's message records.
