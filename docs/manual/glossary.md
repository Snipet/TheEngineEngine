# Glossary

Words the workspaces and this manual use, in plain terms. Where a number is "typical" it is a rule of thumb, not a
law: real engines sit on both sides of it.

**A/R** (a turbine or compressor housing's area over radius). The housing's throat area divided by the distance from
the shaft to the throat's centre. A small A/R speeds the gas onto the wheel: the turbo spools sooner but chokes the
exhaust at high speed. A large A/R spools later and breathes better at the top.

**Air-fuel ratio (AFR)**. The mass of air over the mass of fuel in the charge. Petrol burns completely at about
14.7:1 (stoichiometric); diesel at about 14.5:1. See *lambda*.

**Anti-lag**. With the throttle shut, the ECU retards the spark and enriches the mixture so fuel keeps burning in
the exhaust manifold and spins the turbo. Loud, hard on parts, a rally car's trick.

**BMEP** (brake mean effective pressure). The steady pressure that, pushing on the pistons for one stroke in each
cycle, would give the engine's measured torque. It compares engines of any size: torque (N m) = BMEP (Pa) x
displacement (m³) / 4 pi for a four-stroke. Typical at the torque peak: 10 to 13 bar for a naturally aspirated petrol
engine, 18 to 25 bar turbocharged, 20 to 25 bar a modern truck diesel. IMEP is the same from the gas pressure alone
(before friction), FMEP the part friction takes, PMEP the part spent pumping air in and out.

**Blow-off valve**. A valve between the compressor and the throttle that opens when the throttle shuts under boost,
so the trapped charge escapes instead of pushing back through the compressor (*surge*).

**Bore, stroke, bore/stroke**. The cylinder's diameter and the piston's travel. Over 1 is *oversquare*: a short
stroke, low piston speed, high revs. Under 1 is *undersquare*: long stroke, torque, a lower redline.

**Burn duration, half-burn angle**. How many crank degrees the flame takes to burn the charge (typically 40 to 60
for 10 to 90 % of it), and where half of it is burned (best torque comes at about 8 to 10 degrees after top dead
centre).

**Compression ratio**. The cylinder's volume with the piston at the bottom over its volume at the top. Higher gives
more work from each charge, until the end gas knocks. Typical: 9 to 13 petrol, 15 to 18 a modern diesel.

**Crossplane, flat-plane**. A V8's crank. A flat-plane crank has its throws at 0 and 180 degrees, like two four-
cylinder engines: each bank fires evenly and the exhaust is a high, hard wail; it shakes at twice crank speed. A
crossplane crank has throws at 90 degree steps: smooth, but each bank fires unevenly, which is the burble of a
muscle-car V8 with separate banks.

**Duration** (a cam's). How many crank degrees a valve is open. Quoted "at the seat" (any lift at all) or "at 1 mm"
(0.050 in in America), which is shorter and compares cams better. Street cams have 200 to 230 degrees at 1 mm, race
cams 260 and more.

**ECU** (engine control unit). The computer that decides fuel, spark, boost and cam timing from maps of engine
speed and load, and runs the idle, the limiter, launch control and the rest.

**Firing order, firing interval**. Which cylinder fires after which, and how many crank degrees apart. Even
intervals (720 / the number of cylinders) sound smooth; uneven ones (a crossplane V8's banks, a 90 degree V-twin, a
boxer four with unequal headers) give the lope and the burble.

**Flywheel, inertia**. The crank's resistance to changing speed. A light flywheel revs quickly and stalls easily.

**Knock**. The last of the charge self-igniting before the flame reaches it: a sharp pressure ring that can break
pistons. More advance, more boost, more compression, hotter air and lower-octane fuel all bring it closer. The ECU
listens for it and retards the spark of the cylinder that knocked.

**Lambda**. The air-fuel ratio over the stoichiometric one. 1 burns all the fuel and all the oxygen. Below 1 is
rich (more fuel than the air can burn: about 0.85 to 0.90 gives the most power and keeps the exhaust cooler), above
1 lean (economy, hotter, nearer to knock). Diesels always run lean, 1.3 to 3.

**Lift**. How far a valve opens, in millimetres. Flow grows with lift until about a quarter of the valve's diameter.

**Limiter**. What stops the engine revving past its redline: by cutting the fuel, cutting the spark (which bangs
in the exhaust), retarding the spark (soft) or closing the throttle.

**Lobe separation angle (LSA)**. The angle between the intake and exhaust lobes' centres on the cam, in cam degrees.
Narrow (104 to 108) means more *overlap*: a lumpy idle and a strong mid-range. Wide (112 to 116) means a smooth idle
and a broad, mild curve.

**Overlap**. The crank degrees around top dead centre when both valves are open. The exhaust's outgoing pulse helps
draw the new charge in at high speed; at idle the exhaust flows back into the intake, which is the lope of a hot cam.

**Overrun**. The engine driven by the car with the throttle shut. The ECU cuts the fuel; any that gets through can
burn in the hot pipes as pops and bangs.

**Piston speed**. The piston's mean speed, 2 x stroke x revolutions a second. About 20 to 25 m/s is the limit of a
road engine (friction rises with it and the valvetrain and rods run out of strength); racing engines run 25 m/s and
more.

**Plenum, runners**. The intake's chamber after the throttle and the pipes from it to each port. A runner's length
tunes where its pressure wave helps fill the cylinder (*ram*): long runners for low-speed torque, short for top end.

**Redline**. The highest speed the engine is meant to run at. The limiter sits at or just above it.

**Spark advance**. How many crank degrees before top dead centre the plug fires. The best is the least advance that
gives the most torque (MBT); more knocks, less wastes work as heat in the exhaust.

**Specific output**. Power per litre of displacement: 40 to 75 kW a litre for a naturally aspirated road engine,
75 to 130 turbocharged, more for racing engines.

**Spool**. A turbo's speeding up. *Lag* is the time it takes; the *boost threshold* is the lowest engine speed at
which it makes boost at full throttle.

**Surge**. A compressor asked for pressure at too little flow: the air stalls in it and flows backwards in pulses,
a fluttering "chuff" you hear when a throttle shuts on boost without a blow-off valve.

**Torque, power**. Torque is the crank's twist (N m); power is torque times speed (kW = N m x rpm / 9549). Torque
decides how hard a car pulls in a gear; power, how fast it can go.

**Volumetric efficiency (VE)**. The air the cylinder takes in over the air its swept volume would hold at the
outside air's pressure and temperature. 0.85 to 0.95 for a good naturally aspirated engine at its torque peak, over
1 with tuned runners or boost. Its curve over speed is the torque curve's shape.

**Wastegate**. A valve that lets exhaust bypass the turbine so the boost stops rising at its target. Its *spring*
sets the least boost it holds; the ECU can hold it shut for more.

**Wheel horsepower, crank power**. The engine dyno measures at the crank; a chassis dyno at the wheels, some 10 to
20 % less (the gearbox, the differential, the tyres).
